# Bilingual English / Thai Guidelines

- English and Thai are first-class versions, not shortened or secondary copies.
- Preserve equivalent meaning, project status, limitations, specifications, safety notices, calls to action and navigation.
- Use professional natural Thai; do not translate technical claims into stronger claims than the English source.
- Keep canonical project names and model identifiers stable; explain them in Thai where useful.
- Use Noto Sans Thai or a suitable system fallback; test tone marks, vowel placement, line height and wrapping.
- Provide reciprocal language links and correct lang attributes (en and th).
- Use reciprocal hreflang/canonical metadata where pages are true language equivalents.
- Localise dates, units and contact wording appropriately without changing technical values.
- Maintain one shared content source or an explicit parity checklist to prevent drift.
- Before a release, compare all changed English and Thai sections side by side.
