# Product Design Guidelines

- Design from a defined user problem, operating environment and measurable requirements.
- Prioritise usability, repairability, durability, accessibility, cleaning and maintainability.
- Document intended users, use cases, non-use cases, dimensions, mass targets, payload targets and environmental limits.
- Separate target specifications from measured results.
- Use consistent product naming, model identifiers, labels, warning hierarchy, manuals and QR-linked support information.
- Consider manufacturing process, material availability, assembly, fasteners, service access, replaceable modules, packaging and end-of-life handling.
- Assess water ingress, dust, heat, cold, humidity, UV, corrosion, impact and fire/electrical hazards as relevant. Do not claim IP/fire ratings without suitable tests and standards-based evidence.
- Provide accessible controls, clear indicators and manual override where appropriate.
- Product imagery must distinguish concept renders from photographs of actual prototypes.
- Avoid decorative complexity that increases cost, weight, failure points or maintenance.
