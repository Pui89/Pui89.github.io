# PUI89 Coconut Orchard Intelligence — Early Pilot Brief

**Status:** early product discovery and prototype validation. This brief describes a proposed, limited-scope pilot—not a currently validated commercial product or a guarantee of results.

## The problem we want to investigate

Coconut farm operators and agricultural service providers may need a clearer, more consistent way to record orchard observations and organize harvest-planning information. We want to learn whether a lightweight, human-reviewed digital workflow can improve that process enough to be useful.

We are not assuming that every farm has this problem or that software will necessarily save time or money. Customer discovery comes first.

## Proposed pilot scope

A small, agreed trial may include:

1. Review a sample of orchard images supplied by the partner with documented permission.
2. Record visible bunch/candidate observations and uncertain cases; any automated detections are experimental unless separately validated.
3. Produce a structured report for human review.
4. Compare the assisted workflow with the partner's current process using an agreed protocol.
5. Share a short results note that includes sample size, errors, limitations, and next-step recommendations.

The exact deliverable must be agreed before work begins and must match functions that have actually been implemented.

## What we ask from a pilot partner

- A real workflow problem and a staff contact who understands it.
- Permission to use the selected images/data for the agreed purpose.
- A description of the current process and how its effort or quality is measured.
- Time to review the output and identify mistakes.
- Agreement on data retention, confidentiality, responsibilities, and stopping conditions.

A partner does not need to provide public data or allow images to be published. No data should be uploaded to third-party services without explicit permission.

## How we would evaluate value

Depending on the agreed use case, measures may include:

- Time needed to prepare an inspection record.
- Agreement between system-assisted records and human-reviewed references.
- Missed observations, false detections, and cases requiring correction.
- Time spent on review and report preparation.
- Setup/support effort and any cost required to deliver the workflow.
- Operator feedback on whether the output changes a real decision.

We will not publish performance or savings percentages unless measured and agreed for disclosure. Negative or inconclusive results are valid outcomes.

## What is not included

- Guaranteed yield, maturity, labour-saving, return-on-investment, or accuracy claims.
- A field-validated autonomous harvesting robot.
- Physical cutting, manipulation, or control of machinery.
- Guaranteed detection across all cultivars, lighting, occlusion, tree heights, or orchards.
- Emergency-response, enforcement, or other consequential decisions without suitable specialist validation.

**Safety boundary:** this is a software-assisted review concept. It must not issue physical cutting commands. Any future hardware trial would require a separate engineering risk assessment, independent safety controls, and appropriate specialist review.

## Commercial terms

This is an early discovery brief. Scope, data rights, delivery arrangements, support, any fee, and publication permissions must be discussed and agreed in writing before a pilot begins. Do not assume that the pilot is free, paid, available immediately, or guaranteed to be accepted. The current development approach is free-first, and any spending requires explicit approval from the project owner.

## Zero-spend operating rule

**Budget for planned digital development: ฿0 in additional spending.** Use existing equipment, free tiers only when they require no payment method or create no billable exposure, free/open-source software, public documentation, and local tests wherever practical.

- Do not buy hardware, datasets, domains, certificates, subscriptions, credits, or paid support without the project owner's explicit approval.
- Do not enable paid APIs, cloud compute, hosted inference, storage upgrades, or services that can generate usage charges.
- Before using any free service, check quotas, renewal terms, payment-method requirements, and whether overages can be charged. If unclear, choose a local or no-cost alternative.
- Prefer local CPU/GPU execution on existing equipment and GitHub-hosted features within the existing no-additional-spend limits. Never assume a quota or free tier is unlimited or permanent.
- Record any unavoidable cost before a proposed action and stop for approval; never incur it on the owner's behalf.
- Keep customer discovery remote and no-cost where possible; do not promise a free customer pilot that would require travel, equipment, support, or other unbudgeted work.
- Legal registration, tax/accounting advice, insurance, certification, travel, and physical robot fabrication may carry unavoidable costs. They are not authorized by this strategy and must be researched and approved separately before proceeding.

**Hard rule: if an action may create a charge or financial commitment, do not proceed until the owner explicitly approves it.**
