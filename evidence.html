Evidence before claims

Evidence & Progress

A concise index of the evidence behind PUI89's five product concepts: what is currently documented, what remains unproven, and what should be validated next.

Important: A concept page, design requirement, source-code check or successful software workflow does not prove AI accuracy, physical performance, operational safety, field readiness or certification. No product is presented here as field-validated or commercially released.

Six product evidence summaries

These are conservative evidence boundaries, not readiness scores. Detailed records belong with each product's own documentation and test reports.

Implemented — software foundation

01 · Coconut Harvest AI

Implemented scope: software and evaluation-preparation components for coconut maturity assessment. The harvesting workflow remains a proposal.

Not demonstrated: validated detection accuracy, reliable height/distance performance, safe harvesting or field productivity.

Next evidence: traceable real-image datasets, human-reviewed labels, repeatable CPU-based evaluation and controlled component tests.

Simulation scope only · physical validation pending

02 · Extreme Flood Rescue AI

Documented scope: an early flood-rescue concept with a 15 m simulation scope. The 15 m pole concept remains unvalidated; simulation must not be presented as proof of physical reach, load capacity or rescue performance.

Not demonstrated: a working rescue prototype, structural integrity of the poles, buoyancy or stability, safe operation in moving water, payload capacity or field rescue effectiveness.

Next evidence: publish reproducible simulation scenarios and assumptions, define measurable safety limits, then conduct staged, controlled component and prototype tests before any field use.

Implemented — early software foundation

02 · PUI89 AI HOME

Implemented scope: early software foundations for an indoor assistant focused on household-condition awareness and human-reviewed alerts.

Not demonstrated: certified alarm functions, dependable hazard detection, predictive-warning performance or continuous real-world monitoring.

Next evidence: hazard-specific requirements, sensor provenance, repeatable tests, false-alarm analysis and controlled environment trials.

03 · PUI89 AI NOMAD

Implemented scope: early software foundations for a compact, dog-inspired four-wheel outdoor mobile assistant with a practical carry handle, distinct from the indoor four-wheel AI HOME. This is a design direction, not evidence of a built robot.

Not demonstrated: a physical robot, verified locomotion, payload, runtime, environmental protection or field performance.

Next evidence: user-validated dimensions and ergonomics, wheel and handle requirements, mass and power budgets, risk analysis, bench tests and controlled prototype trials.

How to read evidence

Use the label that matches the strongest traceable evidence—not the ambition of the project.

SpecifiedRequirements and acceptance criteria are documented.
Software-testedDefined software checks passed; this says nothing by itself about physical performance.
Bench / simulatedComponent or simulated scenarios were tested under stated conditions.
Field-validatedRepeatable real-world trials support the specific claim. No such status is claimed here.

Source records

Inspect the underlying project documentation and test definitions. Each record should include its date, commit or run identifier, method, result and limitations.
