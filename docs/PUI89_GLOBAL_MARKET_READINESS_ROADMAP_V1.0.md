# PUI89 Global Market Readiness Roadmap v1.0

**Status:** Proposed strategic roadmap  
**Date:** 22 November 2026  
**Scope:** PUI89 and its six active product projects  
**North Star:** Build a globally trusted Industrial AI & Physical Intelligence Platform Company.  
**Strategy:** Build the Core. Prove the Products. Scale Globally.

This document defines a staged approach for preparing PUI89 products and shared technology for international customers. It is a planning standard, not evidence of company registration, completed products, manufacturing operations, customers, certifications, partnerships, or international sales.

## 1. Global ambition

PUI89 is being developed from Thailand with international markets in mind. The long-term goal is to build reusable Industrial AI and Physical Intelligence technology, prove it through useful products, and make validated products available to customers worldwide.

The operating principle is:

> **Develop from Thailand. Engineer for global trust. Prove before selling. Scale through evidence and customer value.**

PUI89 should not attempt to launch every product in every country at once. Shared technology can be developed across the portfolio, but each product must pass its own technical, safety, regulatory, service, and commercial gates.

## 2. Three strategic pillars

### Build the Core

Develop reusable, versioned technology that can support multiple products without erasing their domain-specific requirements.

- Shared AI Core contracts, evidence provenance, uncertainty handling, safety gates, diagnostics, and recovery.
- Hardware-neutral software integration across CPU, GPU, NPU/accelerators, MCU, memory, sensors, and power constraints.
- Open-source-first development with documented licences, provenance, security, resource requirements, and dependency review.
- Stable interfaces, product adapters, offline capability, and graceful degradation.

Shared AI Core is common infrastructure, not a separate product in the six-product portfolio.

### Prove the Products

For each product, maintain a clear evidence chain:

1. User problem and intended customer.
2. Requirements, intended operating environment, and measurable acceptance criteria.
3. Architecture, hazards, privacy, cybersecurity, and regulatory assessment.
4. Implemented software and documented hardware configuration, where applicable.
5. Repeatable tests, failure tests, and simulation with assumptions disclosed.
6. Controlled real-world evaluation with traceable results.
7. Independent review or certification where required.
8. Product-specific release decision and support plan.

A concept, design, simulation, or passing software test must never be represented as proof of physical performance, safety, certification, or commercial readiness.

### Scale Globally

Expand only when a product has demonstrated customer value and can be legally supplied, supported, maintained, and updated in the target market.

Potential routes include direct sales, selected distributors, industrial partnerships, OEM integration, and licensing. The route must be chosen product by product and country by country.

## 3. Thailand-first, globally trusted development

PUI89's proposed operating standard is to prioritise fabrication, assembly, repair, and maintenance in Thailand where technically and commercially feasible.

For imported components and technology:

- Prefer verified manufacturers and suppliers in Germany, the wider EU, the United States, and the United Kingdom, subject to technical suitability and due diligence.
- Maintain traceability for hardware, firmware, software, models, datasets, and critical dependencies.
- Review supplier ownership and control, country of origin, licensing, security, export controls, sanctions, support lifetime, and replacement options.
- Avoid China-origin and China-controlled dependencies in accordance with PUI89's stated policy.
- Design modular interfaces and alternatives to reduce single-vendor lock-in.
- Do not describe a supplier, component, or supply chain as verified until the relevant checks are completed and recorded.

This is a sourcing and engineering requirement; it does not claim that a qualified supply chain or Thai manufacturing capability is already in place.

## 4. Staged international expansion

### Stage 0 — Establish the foundation

- Publish consistent English-first technical information with Thai-language support.
- Maintain one authoritative product scope, product requirements, evidence records, and maturity vocabulary.
- Define intended users, use cases, exclusions, and product-specific hazards.
- Establish a repeatable product-readiness review.
- Identify legal, regulatory, privacy, cybersecurity, import/export, warranty, and service questions requiring specialist review.

**Exit gate:** Product scope, ownership of evidence, intended use, and unresolved risks are documented. No market-readiness claim is made merely because documentation exists.

### Stage 1 — Prove one initial product

- Select one product based on customer need, feasibility, evidence access, safety, cost, and time to a credible demonstration.
- Interview prospective users and obtain feedback before freezing product assumptions.
- Build a bounded demonstrator or software release appropriate to that product's actual maturity.
- Record test conditions, failures, limitations, operating envelope, and reproducible results.
- Test willingness to pay and identify a realistic first customer segment.

**Exit gate:** There is credible product-specific evidence and a defined customer problem. The selected product is not automatically approved for commercial sale.

### Stage 2 — Enter selected international markets

Screen each potential country before committing resources. Initial candidate regions for research may include Southeast Asia, the United Kingdom, the wider EU, and the United States. These are candidate markets, not confirmed launch destinations.

For each country, assess:

- Customer demand, competing solutions, buyer requirements, and procurement pathways.
- Product classification and applicable safety, radio, electrical, machinery, vehicle, data, AI, and environmental rules.
- Import duties, customs, taxes, local representation, product liability, and contractual obligations.
- Language, documentation, labelling, accessibility, and local support expectations.
- Installation, spare parts, maintenance, warranty, returns, and incident response.
- Data residency, privacy, cybersecurity, and cross-border data transfers where relevant.
- Total landed cost, pricing, channel margin, and realistic service economics.

**Exit gate:** A documented country-specific market case, compliance path, support model, and customer validation exist before launch.

### Stage 3 — Scale responsibly

- Expand through selected distributors, service partners, OEMs, and direct enterprise relationships.
- Establish product lifecycle management, versioned updates, vulnerability response, spare-parts planning, and support procedures.
- Monitor field performance, customer outcomes, failures, recalls, and changing regulatory obligations.
- Enter additional countries only when service and compliance capacity can support them.

**Exit gate:** Expansion is supported by repeatable customer demand, reliable delivery, support capacity, and ongoing compliance.

## 5. Six-product portfolio application

Every product uses the same global readiness framework but retains its own domain-specific validation.

| Active product | Initial international customer hypothesis | Product-specific proof required |
|---|---|---|
| Coconut Harvest AI | Plantation operators, agricultural service providers, and agricultural technology partners | Licensed representative imagery, human-reviewed labels, held-out evaluation, field workflow trials, operator safety, and validated harvesting-system limits |
| PUI89 Sentinel | Industrial, infrastructure, and outdoor inspection organisations | Mobility and stability trials, environmental limits, inspection task performance, communications-loss behaviour, battery/endurance evidence, and applicable robot safety assessment |
| PUI89 AI HOME | Home users, property operators, and care-support environments | Reliable indoor operation, privacy controls, human factors, local/offline behaviour, electrical and product safety, and clearly bounded assistance claims |
| PUI89 AI NOMAD | Frequent travellers and mobility-oriented consumers | Suitcase-form ergonomics, stability, handle and wheel durability, luggage compatibility, battery and transport compliance, weight verification, and real traveller usability |
| PUI89 AI Global SUV | Automotive technology partners and vehicle ecosystem stakeholders | Advisory-only system boundaries, automotive-grade integration requirements, functional safety assessment where applicable, cybersecurity, vehicle validation, and OEM acceptance |
| PUI89 AI Step-Through Scooter | Two-wheel mobility users, fleet operators, and mobility partners | Rider-centred usability, road-condition advisory validation, electrical and environmental protection, vehicle integration constraints, and explicit non-autonomous control boundaries |

These are customer hypotheses and proof requirements, not confirmed customer demand or demonstrated capabilities. Each product's current evidence maturity must be checked in its own repository before any external claim.

## 6. Product release and commercial readiness gates

A product may progress only when its evidence supports the next step.

- **G0 — Concept:** user problem and intended use are described.
- **G1 — Specified:** requirements, system boundaries, hazards, and acceptance criteria are documented.
- **G2 — Implemented:** the relevant software or engineering artefact exists and is versioned.
- **G3 — Tested:** repeatable tests have run under stated conditions, including relevant failure cases.
- **G4 — Evaluated:** controlled real-world evaluation supports bounded claims and known limitations.
- **G5 — Market prepared:** target-country compliance, documentation, supply, pricing, warranty, service, and support plans are reviewed.
- **G6 — Released:** authorised release decision is recorded and all required legal and regulatory conditions are met.

A gate is not passed by presentation quality, a concept image, a roadmap, an unverified supplier statement, or a test that does not represent the intended use.

## 7. Commercial model hypotheses

Evaluate the appropriate mix for each product rather than imposing one model across the portfolio:

- Hardware sale with defined configuration and warranty.
- AI software licence or subscription only where customer value, support costs, and operating economics justify it.
- Maintenance, inspection, training, and support services.
- Enterprise deployment, integration, and OEM licensing.
- Updates and lifecycle services with clear security and end-of-support policies.

No price, margin, recurring revenue, or market-size claim should be published without a documented basis.

## 8. Global trust requirements

Before international deployment, maintain product-appropriate evidence for:

- Technical specifications, configuration, intended use, exclusions, and limitations.
- Safety engineering, risk assessment, human oversight, and emergency procedures.
- Cybersecurity, privacy, data handling, software bills of materials, and vulnerability response.
- Hardware, firmware, software, model, and dataset provenance.
- Licensing and intellectual-property rights.
- Supplier due diligence, component traceability, repairability, and replacement planning.
- Testing, field evaluation, calibration, maintenance, warranty, and incident records.
- Country-specific legal and regulatory review.

## 9. Management review dashboard

Review the following for each product at a regular cadence:

1. Customer problem and evidence of demand.
2. Current maturity gate and evidence links.
3. Critical technical and safety blockers.
4. Regulatory and country-entry blockers.
5. Supply-chain provenance and single-source risks.
6. Estimated build, landed, warranty, and service costs.
7. Support capacity and lifecycle obligations.
8. Next measurable milestone, owner, and review date.

Use **Unknown / Not checked** when evidence is missing. Do not replace missing data with optimistic estimates.

## 10. Immediate next actions

1. Create a common global-readiness checklist and apply it to all six product repositories.
2. Compare the six products using customer access, feasibility, evidence availability, safety burden, and time to a credible demonstration.
3. Select one initial product only after that comparison and user validation.
4. Build its evidence-backed demonstrator and customer discovery plan.
5. Select the first target country only after product classification and country-specific compliance screening.

## 11. Public-claims rule

Until independently supported, describe PUI89 as an independent early-stage initiative developing technology and product concepts from Thailand for potential international markets.

Do not claim that PUI89 is a registered company, has completed manufacturing, has certified products, has confirmed distributors or customers, or is already selling worldwide unless those facts become true and are documented.
