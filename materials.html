PUI89 · Engineering supply chain

Hardware & Materials Sourcing

A transparent route from engineering requirements to traceable materials, components and qualified suppliers. We research first, verify origin and compatibility, then request quotations or samples only when approved.

Current status: sourcing workflow and research framework. This page does not mean PUI89 has purchased, received, integrated, tested or validated any listed hardware.

How to obtain a material or component

Define the requirement

Product, subsystem, function, placement, dimensions, load, environment, interfaces and acceptance criteria.

Find exact candidates

Search manufacturer catalogues and authorised distributors. Compare Thailand-based and international suppliers.

Verify origin

Record manufacturer, brand, exact model/part number, country of manufacture and documentary origin evidence.

Contact the supplier

Request datasheets, drawings, stock, MOQ, lead time, warranty, lifecycle support and a formal quotation.

Calculate landed cost

Record quote date, currency, quantity, taxes, freight, insurance, customs, shipping route and Incoterms.

Check engineering fit

Verify mechanical, electrical, software, thermal, environmental and safety compatibility before sample approval.

Test before integration

Use written bench tests and acceptance criteria. Product-level claims require separate safety and environmental validation.

What every sourcing record must show

Identity & provenance

Manufacturer legal name, brand, exact model/part number, manufacturing country, origin evidence and dated source.

Supplier & availability

Supplier name, authorised status, Thailand/international location, public technical contact, stock status, MOQ and lead time.

Price & delivery

Quoted or indicative price, currency, quote date/expiry, freight, taxes, duties, insurance, Incoterms and estimated landed cost.

Compatibility & risk

Mounting, dimensions, voltage/current, peak power, connectors, protocols, drivers, memory, thermal limits, lifecycle and alternatives.

Module-level vs chip-level BOM

Module-level: buy a complete compute board, camera or sensor module with chips already mounted. Chip-level: identify the exact IC inside it from the manufacturer's datasheet, teardown or written supplier evidence. A chip is not a separate purchase when it is already included in the module.

Required evidence for each selected IC

Manufacturer · exact part number and package · function · quantity per module · datasheet and lifecycle status · manufacturer / assembly / test country where documented · origin evidence · authorised supplier · dated price and stock · electrical, thermal, software and safety compatibility.

Supplier enquiry checklist

  • Confirm the supplier is authorised for the exact model.
  • Request the current datasheet, drawing, interface documents and certifications.
  • Ask where the item is manufactured and request written origin evidence.
  • Confirm stock, MOQ, production lead time, warranty, repair route and lifecycle status.
  • Request a formal dated quotation with currency, validity, shipping terms and all additional charges.
  • Use public business contact channels; do not send confidential PUI89 files during initial enquiries.

Selection gates before any purchase

Benchmark the actual model and sensor workload; confirm Linux/RTOS and driver support; check camera interfaces, RAM bandwidth, thermal design, power budget, lifecycle and safety; compare at least one technically credible alternative.

Country-of-origin rule

A brand's headquarters or chip designer location is not proof of wafer fabrication, assembly or final test country. Record each stage only when supported by a manufacturer statement, traceable lot/package marking or written authorised-distributor evidence. If unknown, keep status UNVERIFIED.

No supplier quotes, current stock, landed prices or manufacturing-country claims are made here. Prefer non-Chinese-origin supply only when the specific part and supplied lot have verifiable origin evidence and meet the engineering requirements. Do not treat a candidate as selected or purchase-ready.

Chip pricing and cost method · Read before comparing

No chip models have been approved for the six products, and there are no complete, verifiable quotations for the portfolio. We therefore do not publish figures that could be mistaken for actual purchase prices. The chip list above describes engineering needs, not a purchase order.

Verified price

Publish only when supported by the manufacturer or an authorized distributor, with exact part number, quantity, currency, date/expiry and tax/shipping terms.

Planning estimate

For budgeting only. Show a range, source, date and confidence level. Never label it a quotation or actual purchase price.

Not priced

Use when the model is not selected or no verifiable price source exists. Do not guess; retain the status NOT PRICED.

Price levelEvidence requiredPUI89 treatment

No-double-counting rule: When buying a complete AI compute board, camera, LiDAR, GNSS, IMU or BMS module, count the module price once. Do not add its onboard CPU, memory, CMOS, IMU or power IC again unless evidence shows that the component must be purchased separately.

Pricing, origin and approval rules

  • Quantities are for one research prototype; spares are excluded unless specified.
  • Ranges are planning allowances, not verified current prices. Record exact part number, dated quote, currency, validity, stock, MOQ and lead time.
  • Calculate landed cost separately: item subtotal + freight + insurance + duty + VAT + brokerage + local delivery. Do not invent landed cost without route and quotation.
  • Record manufacturer, exact model, manufacturing country and documentary evidence. Brand headquarters and seller location do not prove origin.
  • Exclude confirmed Chinese-origin candidates from the approved shortlist. Unknown/conflicting origin is UNVERIFIED; never guess.
  • Safety-critical components and mission-specific instruments require product-specific design review, sourcing evidence and validation.
  • No purchase, paid service, supplier commitment or installation is authorised without explicit approval.

Engineering and purchasing boundary

PUI89 does not assume one component fits every product. Safety-critical parts require product-specific review and testing. No purchases, supplier commitments, downloads or installations are made through this sourcing framework without explicit approval. Public pages show only appropriate non-confidential evidence.
