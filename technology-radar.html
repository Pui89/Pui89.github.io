Technology Radar

A focused view of AI and engineering developments that could strengthen PUI89's shared software foundation and seven product concepts. We track practical relevance, evidence, constraints and the next experiment—not hype.

Editorial baseline: 13 November 2026 · Review cadence: monthly · English / Thai

Five technology domains to watch

These are strategic watch areas, not claims that PUI89 has implemented or validated the technologies.

01 / PERCEPTION

Multimodal AI

Priority: High

Vision-language models, RGB-D, thermal, LiDAR and sensor fusion. Focus on grounding outputs in timestamped observations, source provenance and uncertainty.

PUI89 connection: Coconut Harvest AI, Flood Rescue AI and Sentinel.

02 / COMPUTE

Efficient Edge AI

Priority: Highest

Quantization, model sizing, runtime portability, CPU/GPU/NPU/MCU scheduling, memory budgets and graceful fallback. Benchmark latency, RAM and energy on available hardware before choosing a model.

PUI89 connection: Shared AI Core, HOME, NOMAD, Car and Scooter.

Robot perception, mapping, localization, navigation, manipulation and human-supervised action proposals. Separate planning from actuation and validate safety in controlled stages.

How PUI89 evaluates a trend

1. Relevance: Does it solve a defined problem in one or more PUI89 projects?

2. Evidence: Is there a primary paper, official documentation, model card or reproducible benchmark?

3. Feasibility: Is provenance clear, licensing suitable, and can it be evaluated with existing hardware and open-source tools?

4. Test: Define a small baseline and measure accuracy, latency, RAM, robustness and failure behavior as appropriate.

5. Decision: Adopt, continue monitoring or reject—with a short reason and dated evidence.

Status labels are deliberately conservative: Monitoring → Evaluating → Testing → Implemented. “Implemented” requires a verifiable repository change; “Tested” requires a reproducible test record. A trend appearing here does not mean PUI89 has built, deployed, certified or commercially validated it.

AI trends to watch · 2026–2031

A forward-looking watchlist. These topics differ in maturity; inclusion means “worth monitoring”, not that every capability is ready for deployment.

06 / WORKFLOWS

Agentic AI and AI-assisted engineering

Evaluate selectively

Tool-using agents, task planning, code assistance and multi-step workflows. For PUI89, begin with read-only research, test generation and issue triage. Require human approval for writes, merges, credentials and external actions.

Connection: engineering workflow and all repositories.

07 / ACTION

Vision-Language-Action (VLA)

Monitor / Research

Models that connect visual and language context to action representations. Study grounding, latency, failure modes and safe action constraints before considering physical control.

Connection: HOME, NOMAD, Car and Scooter; advisory-only initially.

08 / MODEL EFFICIENCY

Small Language Models (SLMs)

Evaluate selectively

Compact models, distillation, quantization and retrieval-augmented methods. Compare quality, RAM, latency, energy, license and provenance against simpler non-generative baselines.

Connection: Shared AI Core and offline/edge operation.

09 / SPATIAL REASONING

3D spatial intelligence and world models

Monitor

Scene representations, geometry, motion and predictive models of changing environments. Start with measurable depth, localization and tracking tasks rather than broad claims of general world understanding.

Connection: Flood, robotics and mobility awareness.

10 / SCIENCE

AI for scientific and agricultural discovery

Evaluate by use case

AI-assisted measurement, experiment planning, crop monitoring and scientific analysis. Keep domain experts in the loop and preserve data rights, sampling design and ground-truth quality.

Connection: Coconut Harvest AI and agricultural intelligence.

Technology decisions: adopt, evaluate, monitor or avoid

Adopt

Only when there is a clear product need, compatible open-source license, reproducible evidence, acceptable hardware cost and a passing PUI89 test.

Evaluate

Run a small, bounded benchmark against a baseline. Record dataset rights, model provenance, version, RAM, latency, quality and failure cases.

Monitor

Promising but immature or not yet relevant. Track primary research and official releases; do not add implementation work just because a topic is popular.

Avoid

Unclear provenance or licensing, untrusted model sources, unnecessary paid dependencies, unbounded autonomous actions, unsupported safety claims or solutions without a measurable benefit.

Technology experiments · evidence before claims

Experiment record: question · product requirement · baseline · dataset/source rights · hardware · software/model version · exact command/configuration · metrics · failure cases · result · reviewer · date.

Shared AI Core benchmark: compare a small number of CPU-compatible models or runtimes using the same input and task. Record task quality, latency, peak RAM, model size and fallback behavior. Do not assume an accelerator is available.

Perception benchmark: use licensed real images and fixed train/validation/test splits. Record precision, recall, F1 and per-class errors; do not mix simulated and real-world results.

Simulation test: define scenarios and injected faults, record expected safe behavior, and label all results as simulation until independently validated in the real environment.

Current evidence status: this page defines the proposed evaluation method. It does not report new model benchmarks or claim new physical prototypes.
