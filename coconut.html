01 / AGRICULTURE · AI APPLICATION + ROBOTICS CONCEPT

Coconut Harvest AI

A developing AI application for coconut maturity assessment, alongside a proposed harvesting-robot concept focused on safer, evidence-informed orchard workflows.

IN DEVELOPMENTEVIDENCE-LED DESIGNHUMAN OVERSIGHT
Development status · NOT COMPLETE

Software foundations and evaluation tools are in development. The maturity taxonomy is a working design. No validated detector accuracy, field harvesting, robotic reach or physical safety performance is claimed.

01 / Problem & intent

Problem & intent

Harvest timing

Maturity, cultivar, lighting, occlusion and orchard conditions complicate consistent assessment.

Worker safety

Tall palms and manual harvesting can create access and falling-object risks that need careful engineering.

Traceable decisions

Growers need understandable recommendations, uncertainty and a record of why an observation was flagged.

02 / System direction

System direction

AI application direction

Image intake → quality and provenance checks → future maturity estimation → uncertainty and human review → evidence record. Four working maturity categories: young, mature green, yellow and brown-aging. These are proposed labels; classification boundaries require agronomic review and measured validation.

Robot design direction

A proposed harvesting arm would approach, grip and cut a selected coconut bunch. The collection method is configurable: use an optional removable basket to capture the bunch, or use a controlled drop-after-cut approach where a separately assessed clear landing zone and exclusion area make it appropriate. The arm, cutter, basket, lowering/drop control and safety interlocks are design proposals, not built or validated hardware.

03 / What is not finished

What is not finished

This project is still in development. It is not a finished product.

  • No validated all-stage coconut detector or published field accuracy.
  • No verified 0–30 m detection or harvesting capability; this is a long-term design challenge only.
  • No physically validated arm, cutter, basket, lowering mechanism or autonomous harvesting.
  • Dataset rights, annotation quality and independent held-out evaluation remain gating requirements.
04 / Development sequence

Development sequence

01 · Data integrityReview source, licence, labels and tree/session-level leakage.
02 · Baseline evaluationMeasure class-wise errors on a reviewed held-out set.
03 · Decision supportTest uncertainty, rejection and human review.
04 · Robotics studySimulation and safety review before any staged hardware work.

Standards & governance

  • ISO 10218-1:2025 and ISO 10218-2:2025 — industrial robot safety references; applicability must be assessed for the final system.
  • NIST AI Risk Management Framework — AI risk lifecycle guidance.

Perception & Task Intelligence

Project-specific capabilities built on the PUI89 Shared AI Core.

Perception

  • Image quality: blur, lighting, glare and occlusion checks
  • Coconut bunch and fruit detection/localization
  • Four maturity classes: young green, mature green, yellow and brown
  • Fruit/bunch segmentation and counting support
  • Growth and maturity change tracking across dated observations
  • Confidence, unknown class and human-review signals

Tasks

  • Inspect image quality before inference
  • Detect and localize coconut bunches and fruits
  • Classify maturity into the four defined classes
  • Count visible fruits with duplicate/occlusion warnings
  • Compare dated observations for maturity changes
  • Prepare a harvest-priority report for human review

Future research

Real licensed orchard imagery, robust occlusion handling, calibrated confidence, low-memory CPU inference and field evaluation against human labels.

PUI89 · SHARED INTELLIGENCE FOUNDATION

Perception & Task Intelligence (PTI)

Uses the shared PUI89 PTI framework, with modules selected for each project's actual problem

Project-specific perception focus

Coconut maturity and harvest readiness

Shared PUI89 task lifecycle

Image-quality checks; fruit and bunch detection; four maturity classes (young green, mature green, yellow and brown); segmentation and localisation; tracking across views where reliable; occlusion and distance uncertainty.

  1. Observe — Check source, timestamp, input quality and sensor health
  2. Perceive — Detect, classify, segment, track and estimate spatial context when available
  3. Assess — Evaluate context, risk, anomalies and uncertainty
  4. Plan — Propose reviewable options; do not command hardware
  5. Verify — Human review, evidence logging, testing and safe hold/abstention when uncertain

Safety and evidence rules

Assess maturity evidence and harvest context; propose a reviewable harvest-priority record. No autonomous cutting or harvesting is implied.

Every output must identify source, timestamp, model version, confidence and limitations. Unintegrated sensors or models must not be presented as operational.

PUI89 — Seven-Dimension Quality Framework

Last reviewed: 8 November 2026 · Review period: November 2026

Seven practical dimensions guide the quality and maturity of this project. The coloured markers identify the review dimensions; they do not represent a certification or a claim that every recommendation has been implemented.

  1. ● Brand identity
    Keep the PUI89 identity, project naming, typography, Deep Navy and Electric Cyan foundation, and project-specific accent consistent across materials.
  2. ● Website and accessibility
    Maintain accurate English/Thai parity, responsive layouts, clear navigation, accessible contrast, keyboard focus, meaningful links and current project dates.
  3. ● AI software and applications
    Use versioned interfaces, traceable observations, uncertainty-aware recommendations, safe abstention, human approval where consequential, and reproducible software tests.
  4. ● Physical products and robotics
    Define measurable requirements for mechanical safety, power, thermal behaviour, water ingress, fire risk, serviceability and emergency stop; mark each as untested until evidence exists.
  5. ● Documentation and research evidence
    Keep architecture, requirements, test records, data/model provenance, licences, limitations, known gaps and evidence links understandable and current.
  6. ● Product validation and release gates
    Clarify the user problem, intended operator, value, deployment/support needs, lawful data/IP position and validation gates before making commercial claims.
  7. ● Professional environment and presentation
    Prepare consistent technical presentations, diagrams, demonstrations, laboratory/facility requirements and partner-facing materials without implying facilities or partnerships that do not exist.
