AGRONOMIC VISUAL GUIDE

Four working coconut maturity categories

These are proposed project labels for dataset review and agronomic assessment. They are not yet validated model classes; cultivar, intended use, lighting and orchard conditions can affect visible appearance.

01 · Young greenDeveloping fruit; visual cues and intended harvest use require expert-reviewed labels.
02 · Mature greenGreen outer appearance; maturity must not be inferred from colour alone.
03 · YellowYellowing appearance; record variation and avoid assuming a universal maturity boundary.
04 · Brown / agingBrown or aging appearance; distinguish natural maturity from damage and other causes.

Evidence rule: use licensed, traceable real orchard images, reviewed human annotations and a held-out evaluation set before reporting detector performance.

SOFTWARE · WORKFLOW

How the AI inspection workflow is intended to work

This diagram explains the planned processing sequence. It does not show a live detector or claim that each stage is already implemented.

1. Capture and quality

Check image provenance, resolution, blur, lighting, occlusion and whether fruit is sufficiently visible.

2. Assess and express uncertainty

Future computer-vision models may estimate visible classes; insufficient or conflicting evidence must be rejected or flagged.

3. Human review and record

Show evidence and uncertainty to the operator, record corrections and produce a traceable assessment. No automated cutting authorisation.

01 / Problem & intent

Problem & intent

Harvest timing

Maturity, cultivar, lighting, occlusion and orchard conditions complicate consistent assessment.

Worker safety

Tall palms and manual harvesting can create access and falling-object risks that need careful engineering.

Traceable decisions

Growers need understandable recommendations, uncertainty and a record of why an observation was flagged.

02 / System direction

System direction

AI application direction

Image intake → quality and provenance checks → future maturity estimation → uncertainty and human review → evidence record. Four working maturity categories: young, mature green, yellow and brown-aging. These are proposed labels; classification boundaries require agronomic review and measured validation.

Robot design direction

A proposed harvesting arm would approach, grip and cut a selected coconut bunch. The collection method is configurable: use an optional removable basket to capture the bunch, or use a controlled drop-after-cut approach where a separately assessed clear landing zone and exclusion area make it appropriate. The arm, cutter, basket, lowering/drop control and safety interlocks are design proposals, not built or validated hardware.

03 / What is not finished

What is not finished

This project is still in development. It is not a finished product.

  • No verified 0–30 m detection or harvesting capability; this is a long-term design challenge only.
  • No physically validated arm, cutter, basket, lowering mechanism or autonomous harvesting.
  • Dataset rights, annotation quality and independent held-out evaluation remain gating requirements.

PUI89 PRODUCT UPDATE · 18 NOV 2026

How Coconut Harvest AI works

Coconut Harvest AI is an evidence-led agricultural decision-support workflow and a proposed future harvesting robot. It is designed to help growers review visible coconut maturity and prepare traceable harvest assessments.

AI workflow

  1. Capture orchard images and check image quality, blur, lighting and occlusion.
  2. Assess visible bunches and fruit; target four classes: young green, mature green, yellow and brown.
  3. Record uncertainty, source provenance and human corrections.
  4. Prepare a harvest-assessment report for human review; no image score authorises cutting.

Technology direction

Computer vision, optional depth/3D sensing, CPU-first edge processing, dataset provenance checks, human annotation, leakage-safe evaluation and reproducible precision/recall/F1 reporting.

Who it helps

Growers, plantations, agricultural organisations, equipment developers and researchers seeking more consistent inspection and evidence-based harvest planning.

Current boundary: the real-image training/approval gate remains controlled; detector accuracy, 0–30 m perception and physical harvesting are not validated. A future arm/cutter/collection mechanism requires separate engineering and safety validation.

Product Capability Architecture

Perceive → Understand → Predict → Decide → Act. Coconut Harvest AI combines agricultural vision, evidence-led maturity assessment and a proposed future physical-harvesting workflow.

Sensory & perception

  • RGB recognition of palms, bunches and fruit across four visible groups: young green, mature green, yellow and brown.
  • Image quality, occlusion, visible defects, size and shape cues.
  • Optional calibrated depth/3D, spatial mapping, tactile/force, grip/slip, vibration and orchard-environment sensing.

Understanding & prediction

  • Uncertainty-aware maturity assessment, human-reviewed harvest recommendations and traceable evidence.
  • Temporal fruit/plant change tracking; harvest-window prediction research requires longitudinal labelled data.
  • Arm/gripper task planning and digital-twin simulation before physical work.

Physical intelligence & reliability

Future supervised grasp/cut workflow, sensor-health checks, safe hold, fault reporting, CPU-first edge inference and abstention when evidence is insufficient.

Evidence boundary: no validated orchard detector, approved training dataset, 0–30 m perception, autonomous cutting, tree climbing or physical harvesting robot is claimed. Concept and research targets are not field results.

PUI89 · LOCAL-FIRST USER EXPERIENCE

On-device harvest warnings & machine status

Essential warnings and system status must appear directly on the product's integrated display or its appropriate local operator station. A smartphone app, account, pairing, internet connection or remote service must never be required for critical alerts, safe stopping or essential recovery.

Four alert severity levels

Information · Advisory · Warning · Emergency

Each message should identify what happened, the affected area or subsystem, event time and source, confidence or uncertainty, the recommended safe action, and whether human acknowledgement is needed. Missing, stale or conflicting evidence must never be presented as confirmed or safe.

Product-specific warning scope

  • Harvest workflow and image-quality issues; uncertain or conflicting maturity assessments; target/fruit visibility and sensing limitations; machine readiness, guarded-area and operator-confirmation states; power, communication or equipment faults.
  • A maturity estimate is decision support only. It must never authorise cutting, climbing or machine motion; human confirmation and independent machine safeguards remain mandatory.

Local history & resilience

Keep active alerts and timestamped history locally, including acknowledgement and cleared state. Essential alerts should continue during internet/cloud loss where technically feasible. Optional mobile notifications may only mirror local alerts. Report sensor obstruction, stale data, communication loss and internal faults; define a safe response and never auto-resume hazardous operation.

Design requirement only — not evidence that a display, alert system or hardware has been installed, tested or certified. Product conformance remains Unknown / Not Tested until traceable evidence exists.

PUI89 · INCIDENT EVIDENCE & HISTORY

Incident records & saved-image recall

When Coconut Harvest AI detects an abnormal condition, create a local incident record. Product-specific evidence may include: Harvest observations, fruit/bunch visibility, maturity uncertainty, machine readiness and guarded-work-zone events. A maturity estimate is decision support only and never authorises cutting or machine motion.

Text, summary, voice and images

Show a concise warning and event summary on the product display, with audible tones and spoken alerts appropriate to severity. Users can press to read full details, inspect saved evidence images and replay the voice alert from the product display or local console. If no image was actually captured, say so; never generate an image and present it as evidence.

Browse by date

Provide READ / VIEW HISTORY with year → month → day navigation. Users can open summaries, read full alerts, view saved images and browse earlier/later incidents. Every image shows event date, time and incident ID, plus clear labels for incomplete or uncertain data.

Evidence stored locally

History must be reviewable without a phone, app or internet. Warn when storage is nearing capacity; never automatically delete or overwrite important evidence. Export and recovery remain user-controlled.

Design requirement only. Image capture, date-based recall, voice replay and evidence preservation remain Unknown / Not Tested until verified.

Offline Independence & Connectivity Resilience

The product must work without the app. Safety must work without connectivity. The QR code, smartphone app, Wi-Fi, internet and cloud services are optional additions—not prerequisites for reading essential instructions, supported basic local operation, local stop/emergency stop or safe shutdown.

  • Printed documentation: product-specific Quick Start and Safety Guide is supplied with the unit; the complete manual is available as a physically supplied or otherwise offline-readable document. QR and a readable short URL are supplementary only.
  • Local controls: essential local controls, status/fault indication and applicable emergency-stop path do not depend on a phone, app, network or cloud.
  • Offline behaviour: each product defines which functions operate locally, which need an optional local connection, and which are internet-dependent. Unavailable AI or stale data must be clearly identified, never guessed.
  • Connectivity loss: remote commands are disabled when the link or data freshness is uncertain. The product follows its own hazard-based safe-state design and never resumes hazardous activity automatically on reconnection.
  • Recovery: local records, restart behaviour, power-loss handling, troubleshooting and authorised service procedures are documented.
  • Remote Companion: optional monitoring and support only; it cannot bypass physical controls, interlocks, emergency stops or human supervision.

These are proposed engineering requirements, not claims of implemented or validated capabilities. See the PUI89 Offline Independence & Connectivity Resilience Standard v1.0. Product-specific implementation and test evidence must be reviewed before any customer-facing capability claim.

In-box printed manual requirement: the complete printed User Manual and printed Quick Start & Safety Guide must be supplied inside the product box. Both must be readable immediately without a smartphone, QR scan, account, app, Wi-Fi or internet. QR and URL are supplementary only.
