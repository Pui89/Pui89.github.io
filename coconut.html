01 / AGRICULTURE

Coconut Harvest AI

An AI application for assessing coconut maturity, paired with a proposed agricultural robot concept designed around safer, more informed harvest workflows.

Current status: Software and concept development. The robot is a design concept, not a physically validated harvesting machine. No field accuracy or harvesting performance is claimed.

The challenge

Coconut production involves decisions about maturity, access, worker safety and harvest timing. Tree height, lighting, occlusion, cultivar differences and field conditions make reliable assessment difficult.

AI application

The project organises visual maturity assessment into four working categories: young green, developing green, mature green and brown-aging. The intended workflow is to capture observations, assess image quality and uncertainty, produce a reviewable maturity estimate, and record the evidence behind each result.

Any model performance claim must be supported by documented data provenance, permitted image use, independent evaluation splits and measured metrics such as precision, recall, class-wise performance and inference time.

Robotics concept

The longer-term concept explores a stable agricultural platform with perception sensors, localisation, safe positioning and a controlled harvesting and capture mechanism. A 0–30 m tree-height range is a design challenge for future study—not a demonstrated sensing, reach or harvesting specification.

Development and validation path

  1. Establish lawful, traceable and representative image data.
  2. Test simple baselines and document class-level errors.
  3. Evaluate uncertainty, image-quality rejection and human review.
  4. Develop a simulation and mock task-planning interface.
  5. Only after engineering and safety reviews, consider supervised, staged hardware trials.

Who can help

Growers and agricultural researchers can help define useful maturity labels and field conditions. Computer-vision engineers can review dataset design and evaluation. Robotics specialists can assess reach, stability, cutting, capture and fail-safe requirements. Partners can support clearly scoped trials or equipment access.

Engineering standards and safety framework

The project uses recognised references to guide future design review: ISO 12100 for machinery risk assessment, ISO 10218-1:2025 and ISO 10218-2:2025 for relevant industrial robot safety topics, and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework for AI risk management.

These are design references only—not certification or a claim of compliance. Qualified specialists must determine which standards and Thai/target-market requirements apply to the final machine. Current software checks do not validate a physical arm, cutter, basket, lowering mechanism, 0–30 m reach, or field safety.
