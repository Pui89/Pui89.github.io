02 / DISASTER RESPONSE · AI APPLICATION + ROBOTICS CONCEPT

Extreme Flood Rescue AI

A developing AI decision-support concept for flood situational awareness, hazard mapping and human-supervised assistance planning, with proposed land-and-water rescue platforms.

IN DEVELOPMENTEVIDENCE-LED DESIGNHUMAN OVERSIGHT
Development status · NOT COMPLETE

Software checks and scenario/report tooling are under development. The 0–15 m flood range is a scenario/design target only. No validated flood-depth operating envelope, navigation, buoyancy, payload, rescue or field performance is claimed.

01 / Problem & intent

Problem & intent

Disrupted access

Flooding can isolate communities, block roads and damage communications.

Changing hazards

Current, debris, water depth, visibility and structural damage can change quickly.

Responder safety

Technology must support trained rescue teams, not create new hazards or replace their judgement.

02 / System direction

System direction

AI application direction

Combine permitted visual observations, map context and environmental inputs; track freshness and uncertainty; produce reviewable hazard summaries and assistance priorities for trained responders.

Rescue robotics direction

Study aerial reconnaissance and a boat-shaped amphibious platform with retractable wheels for suitable land/shallow-water transitions. Passenger/animal space, payload and propulsion remain unverified design requirements.

Platform architecture & mission envelope

Coordinated platforms, separate operating limits

Mission scenarios

Support planning and evaluation across 0–15 m flood scenarios, including severe urban, rural and mountain-flood environments. The depth range describes scenario coverage for research and simulation; it is not a certified operating depth for any vehicle or sensor.

Platform strategy

  • Aerial systems for reconnaissance and broad-area observation.
  • Suitable surface craft for water-based assessment and assistance.
  • Ground and amphibious robots for terrain and shallow-water conditions within their own limits.
  • A separately engineered future submersible for suitable underwater reconnaissance, only after dedicated design and validation.

AI decision support

Explore person and animal candidate detection, flood-scene understanding, hazard mapping, route proposals, changing-condition awareness, uncertainty reporting and operator alerts. Outputs are proposals for trained responders to review, not guaranteed detections or autonomous rescue decisions.

Safety rule: Each platform must remain within its own evidence-backed operating envelope. Unknown, conflicting or unsafe conditions trigger hold, replan or human review. A 0–15 m scenario target does not mean every platform can enter or operate at every depth.

03 / What is not finished

What is not finished

This project is still in development. It is not a finished product.

  • No validated flood perception, water-depth estimation, SLAM or autonomous navigation performance.
  • 0–15 m describes a research/simulation scenario range, not a submergence rating or guaranteed operating depth.
  • No built or validated amphibious robot, buoyancy, stability, propulsion, payload or people/animal transport capability.
  • Simulation and software tests do not prove real flood rescue effectiveness.

Development sequence

03 · SimulationRecord simulator version, seeds, routes, collisions and failures.
04 · Engineering reviewAssess buoyancy, stability, propulsion and staged unmanned trials.
05 / Standards & governance

Standards & governance

Safety and responsible deployment

Use ISO 12100 as a machinery risk-assessment reference where applicable and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework for AI risk management. Applicable marine, electrical, radio, occupational and emergency-response requirements must be identified by qualified specialists for the actual platform and jurisdiction.

These are references, not certification. No person or animal should be carried in an experimental platform before independent engineering review and staged safety validation.

PUI89 — Seven-Dimension Quality Framework

Coloured markers identify improvement dimensions, not certification or completed implementation.

  1. ● Brand identity
    Consistent PUI89 naming, typography, Deep Navy/Electric Cyan identity and project accent.
  2. ● Website and accessibility
    Accurate EN/TH parity, responsive pages, accessible contrast, keyboard navigation, clear links and current dates.

    Measurable safety requirements for structure, power, heat, water ingress, fire risk, maintenance and emergency stop; mark untested items honestly.
  5. ● Documentation and research evidence
    Current architecture, requirements, tests, data/model provenance, licences, limitations and evidence links.
  6. ● Product and commercial readiness
    Clear user problem, intended operator, value, support, lawful data/IP and validation gates before commercial claims.

    Consistent technical presentations, diagrams, demonstrations and facility requirements without implying unverified partnerships or facilities.
